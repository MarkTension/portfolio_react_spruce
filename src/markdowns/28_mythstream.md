# New Album - MythStream

<img width= "90%" src="/images/mythstream.jpeg" alt="MythStream album cover">

MythStream got released yesterday, May 29'th 2026.

For all IDM nerds out there, May 29 shares the release day with Boards of Canada’s new album, Inferno. 
BoC was my first deep love for music that felt more personal to me. Most music I listened to before was shared with my parents or in popular culture, but this was my own thing. It was less approachable, more weird, and the perfect portal to take my head into new places. It’s like being allowed to experience something far away from the human world, away from culture and social dynamics. Just raw experience of whatever. I guess that is what IDM is for me.
I haven’t listened to Inferno yet, but jonesing for the right moment to have an uninterrupted session.

## What is the inspiration for MythStream?
MythStream comes from this same love for IDM. A bit alien, but familiar. Heavily influenced by the sounds I grew up with: Jungle soundtracks from space racer video games, endlessly watching my friend play Rayman 2, and stretched-out startup sounds of Windows 95.
But then tied together with what I love about the music from now: Purelink’s merging of club and ambient, Deconstructed club music from the West Mineral Ltd. label, Skee Mask, and influences from friends making music, such as Griff’s Aspetuck project, or lmnop’s project of Casper, who was super involved in making this album as well. 

The label it's released on, Never Late, is quite new and covers a really fun corner of music.

The accompanying video was made by sampling frames from my [Microcosmos](/blog/microcosmos-differentiable-artificial-life) simulations and cutting them to the music with a small config-driven editor. That process is what changed my mind about where digital art is heading, which I wrote about in [Future of digital art is generative models or code?](/blog/future-of-digital-art).
